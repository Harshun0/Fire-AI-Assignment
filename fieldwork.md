## Conversation
Name: Rahul
Role: B.Tech student, runs an Instagram page

Three things he said:
1. "A follow-up tracker is actually more useful for creators than for job applications —
   for a job you don't really need to follow up with a company that much."
2. "With creators it's different — there are so many mails going out and so many responses
   coming back that I have to manually check each one to know where things stand."
3. "I just open the mail and check it myself every time, there's no system for it."

One thing that surprised me:
I went in expecting to confirm the job cold-mail follow-up problem from research.md. But
Rahul said follow-up isn't really a pain point for job applications — it's a much bigger
problem for his creator outreach, because of the sheer volume of mails and replies he has
to manually track. That's what actually changed the plan: the tool is built for tracking
his creator-hiring outreach, not his own job applications.

## Data collection note
How I collected `data.csv`: sat with Rahul at his home from 6:30-7:00 PM, opened his mail
together, and created the data as a CSV file directly — went through each creator he had
contacted and logged the creator's name, platform, date contacted, whether he had already
followed up, and the current status (pending / rejected / selected).

Known gaps in the data: all 40 rows share the same contacted_on date (16 Nov 2026) and the
same status ("Contacted") — this is genuine, not rounded. Rahul sent his outreach to all 40
creators in one sitting on that day, and as of today (21 Nov) nobody has replied yet, so
there are no "rejected"/"selected" rows in the data at all. A couple of creator handles in
the sheet are also partial/truncated (e.g. "kareenaf...") because that's how he'd noted them
down while going through his DMs quickly — I kept them as-is rather than guessing the rest.
